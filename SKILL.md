# Local SEO outreach

Offer a small business something useful and free: a one-page report on how findable it is, with the single
biggest gap named plainly. The gap leads to a matching offer. The report earns the conversation; it is not
the pitch.

All names, sites and figures in this skill and its references are invented placeholders. Use real ones only
inside the user's own working files, never in anything you publish.

## Hard rules

1. **Benefit first.** The first message says what the business gets, in its terms: more calls, more
   customers found, less time lost. It does not ask the reader to do work for you.
2. **Small, optional ask.** One easy step they can ignore: "want me to send the one-page report?" Not "book a
   30 minute call" and not "test our product and tell us what is missing".
3. **No apology line, no opt-out line at the end.** Do not close with "sorry to bother you", "ignore this if
   not relevant", or "reply 'stop' and I won't write again". Those lines signal that the sender expects to
   be unwelcome. Where the law requires an opt-out path, provide it through the sending system (unsubscribe
   header or footer), not in the closing sentence of the body.
4. **End with a question and a signature.** The last line of the body is one question about the next small
   step. Then the sender's name and business, nothing after.
5. **No income promises.** No "double your leads", no percentages, no guaranteed rankings. State what you
   observed, not what will happen.
6. **Claim only what you checked.** If a source was unavailable or a tool does not mirror what the customer
   sees, say so in the report. "In our own web search you were not in the first results; Google's ranking
   may differ" is acceptable. "You do not appear on Google" is not.
7. **A human approves every send.** The agent drafts, groups, and schedules proposals. A person reads each
   message and presses send, or explicitly approves a batch. No autonomous sending, no account creation, no
   paid calls without a stated cap.

## Workflow

1. **Pick one narrow vertical** (for example electrical wholesalers, vet clinics, driving schools). One
   vertical makes the audit method, the report and the message reusable. Test on about 20 businesses before
   widening.
2. **Select leads.** From the user's own lead source: valid contact address, not already contacted, not on
   any suppression list, a business (not a person) in the target vertical. Remove chains, manufacturers,
   franchises and sole traders when the local law treats them differently. Spread across cities if you want
   to learn what varies by place. Keep a reserve list of unvetted backups.
3. **Check the law for each country before the first send.** B2B cold email is allowed in some places, needs
   prior consent in others, and some regimes treat sole traders like consumers. Record the rule, the source,
   and whether you verified it; mark anything unverified as unverified. This is not legal advice: the user
   decides, ideally with a local professional. See `references/outreach-expansion.md`.
4. **Run the mini audit** per business, using free methods only (`references/audit-method.md`).
5. **Write the one-page report** from `references/one-pager-template.md`: three or four observations, one
   biggest gap, one matching offer. No numeric promises.
6. **Draft the first message and the follow-up** with `references/message-patterns.md`.
7. **Lint every draft** against the hard rules, then hand the batch to the human with the report attached or
   linked.
8. **Log replies and learn.** Record who replied, what they asked, and what they objected to. Update the
   pattern notes with the evidence and the sample size.

## Mini audit, in short

For each business collect, from public pages only: website basics (mobile viewport, title and meta, a
tappable phone number, WhatsApp or contact form, https redirect, footer year, structured data types,
broken menu links), search presence (a web search for "<city> <vertical>" and the business name, noting
what shows), maps presence (rating and review count with the capture date), and whether an AI assistant
with web search names the business for "best <vertical> in <city>". Record each result with the date and
the exact method. Full steps: `references/audit-method.md`.

## Choosing the one gap and the one offer

Pick the observation a business owner would most readily agree is a problem and that connects to something
you can actually deliver:

| Biggest gap | Offer it connects to |
|---|---|
| No catalog or product pages, thin site | Website with a product catalog |
| Not mobile friendly, empty title, broken pages | Website fix |
| Few or no reviews, incomplete map listing, missing hours or category | Google Business Profile setup |
| Customers cannot check stock or find equivalents online | Stock and equivalent-product search tool |
| Absent from AI assistant answers while competitors appear | Local content and listing clean-up |

Name one. A long list of faults reads as an attack.

## What a good first message looks like

Short, in the reader's language, one idea. Greeting, one sentence about what you noticed, the benefit, the
small ask as a question, signature. Example with invented names:

```
Hello Example Electric team,

I looked at how a customer in Sampletown finds an electrical wholesaler online. Your shop has solid
reviews, but your website has no product catalog, so people who search for a specific cable cannot see that
you stock it.

I wrote a one-page report on this, free. Would you like me to send it?

Jane Doe
Example Studio
```

More patterns, follow-up timing and the evidence behind the benefit-first rule are in
`references/message-patterns.md`.

## Before you hand over a batch

- Each message names something true and specific about that business.
- No promise of results, no invented urgency.
- Last line is a question, then the signature, nothing after.
- Reports contain no claim you could not reproduce today.
- Legal status for each recipient's country is recorded.
- The human has the list and the final say.
