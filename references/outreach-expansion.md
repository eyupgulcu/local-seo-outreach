# Expanding outreach to new countries or segments

Treat expansion as research first, sending second. This is a method, not legal advice. Nothing here replaces
a local professional or the regulator's own pages.

## 1. Record the legal position per country

For each candidate country, keep a small table:

| Country | B2B cold email | Conditions | Language | Source and date | Verified? | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home country | allowed to companies and traders | recipient opt-out must be honored; sender identity in the message | local | regulator page, 2026-01 | yes | active |
| Country B | allowed to limited companies, not sole traders | valid reply address, suppression list | English | regulator guidance, 2026-01 | partly | pilot |
| Country C | prior consent required | none | n/a | vendor blog, 2026-01 | no | exclude |

Rules for the table:

- Prefer the regulator's or the statute's own page. Vendor blogs are secondary; mark them as such.
- Mark anything unconfirmed "not verified". Do not turn a guess into a green light.
- Where consent is required for B2B, exclude the country unless the user takes advice and decides
  otherwise.
- Sole traders and generic personal mailbox domains are often treated like consumers. Filter them out when
  the country's rule differs by company type.
- Apply the country of the recipient's address, not the owner's ethnicity or language.
- Check whether the recipient already exercised an opt-out right, and keep a permanent suppression list. In
  some countries you must register opt-outs with an official system within a deadline.

## 2. Rank markets

Weigh: legal clarity, language you can write well, how closely the business culture matches yours, how
reachable decision makers are on the channel you use (email or chat), payment and contract risk, and whether
you have an offer that fits. A market with unclear law and risky payment goes to the end of the list even if
it is large.

## 3. Choose segments within a market

Pick segments where your offer is obvious (the one-gap-one-offer table in the main skill). Write the search
queries you would use to build the lead list for each segment and city, in the local language and in English.
Do not run paid scrapes until the user approves a cap.

## 4. Pilot

Start with a small batch per country (for example 20), human-approved, with the lowest-risk template. Record
replies, objections, bounces and opt-outs. Expand only if the batch is clean and the law check holds.

## 5. Keep the human in charge

The agent prepares tables, lists and drafts. The human decides which countries open, approves each batch,
and presses send.
